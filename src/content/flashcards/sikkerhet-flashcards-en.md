# Cybersikkerhet puggekort

## Card 1

**Q:** In cyber security, the term for a person or entity that may perform a potentially harmful action is a/an _____.

**A:** Threat actor

---

## Card 2

**Q:** According to the IIA Norway material, what is the average cost of a data breach?

**A:** 43 million NOK.

---

## Card 3

**Q:** What percentage of all cyber attacks start with a phishing email or a link click?

**A:** 75%.

---

## Card 4

**Q:** Which specific type of malware is described by NSM as the 'dominant' threat against Norwegian organizations?

**A:** Ransomware.

---

## Card 5

**Q:** Of the organizations that pay a ransom after an attack, what percentage actually recover all their data?

**A:** 8%.

---

## Card 6

**Q:** What are the four broad categories of threat actors identified by the IIA Norway materials?

**A:** Intelligence agencies, criminals, activists, and terrorists.

---

## Card 7

**Q:** Under which category do 'insiders' fall if they perform a cyber attack?

**A:** They are categorized based on their specific motive and who they work for.

---

## Card 8

**Q:** Which five motives are commonly attributed to threat actors in the provided materials?

**A:** Sabotage, espionage, influence, financial gain, and infrastructure.

---

## Card 9

**Q:** NSM identifies four main categories for its 'Fundamental Principles for ICT Security'. Category 1 is _____.

**A:** Identify and map (Identifisere og kartlegge).

---

## Card 10

**Q:** NSM identifies four main categories for its 'Fundamental Principles for ICT Security'. Category 2 is _____.

**A:** Protect and maintain (Beskytte og oppretthold).

---

## Card 11

**Q:** NSM identifies four main categories for its 'Fundamental Principles for ICT Security'. Category 3 is _____.

**A:** Detect (Oppdag).

---

## Card 12

**Q:** NSM identifies four main categories for its 'Fundamental Principles for ICT Security'. Category 4 is _____.

**A:** Handle and recover (Håndter og gjenopprett).

---

## Card 13

**Q:** In the context of risk assessment, 'Primary assets' consist of information and _____.

**A:** Business processes.

---

## Card 14

**Q:** The assets that enable or support primary assets, such as hardware and personnel, are known as _____.

**A:** Secondary assets (støtteverdier).

---

## Card 15

**Q:** Concept: Risk assessment (Risikovurdering)

**A:** Definition: The process of identifying, analyzing, and evaluating risks to ICT systems.

---

## Card 16

**Q:** Why should documentation regarding an organization's risk assessment be treated as sensitive information?

**A:** It describes vulnerabilities and business-critical details that could be exploited.

---

## Card 17

**Q:** The standard model for calculating risk can be expressed as $Risk = f(Threat, Vulnerability, Impact)$.

**A:** True.

---

## Card 18

**Q:** What is the primary tool for controlling network traffic between different security zones?

**A:** A firewall.

---

## Card 19

**Q:** A firewall that ignores the context of a connection and only checks individual packets is called a _____ firewall.

**A:** Stateless (or packet filtering).

---

## Card 20

**Q:** Term: Stateful inspection

**A:** Definition: A firewall technology that monitors the state of active connections to distinguish legitimate return traffic from unwanted incoming traffic.

---

## Card 21

**Q:** Which type of firewall is specifically designed to inspect HTTP/HTTPS traffic to block SQL injections and Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)?

**A:** WAF (Web Application Firewall).

---

## Card 22

**Q:** The security principle of blocking all traffic by default and only allowing documented necessary traffic is called _____.

**A:** Default-deny.

---

## Card 23

**Q:** How does an IPS (Intrusion Prevention System) differ from an IDS (Intrusion Detection System)?

**A:** An IPS automatically blocks suspicious traffic, while an IDS only monitors and alerts.

---

## Card 24

**Q:** What is the purpose of a DMZ (Demilitarized Zone) in network architecture?

**A:** To isolate internet-exposed servers from the internal network.

---

## Card 25

**Q:** The process of dividing an internal network into logical zones to prevent an attacker's movement is called network _____.

**A:** Segmentation.

---

## Card 26

**Q:** Term: Lateral movement

**A:** Definition: The ability of an attacker to move between systems within a network after gaining initial access.

---

## Card 27

**Q:** Which technology ensures that data remains confidential even if it falls into the wrong hands?

**A:** Encryption.

---

## Card 28

**Q:** Kryptering: Symmetrisk vs. Asymmetrisk. Symmetrisk kryptering bruker _____ til både kryptering og dekryptering.

**A:** Den samme nøkkelen (the same key).

---

## Card 29

**Q:** Asymmetric encryption uses a key pair consisting of a private key and a/an _____.

**A:** Public key.

---

## Card 30

**Q:** The current industry standard for symmetric encryption of data at rest is _____.

**A:** AES-256.

---

## Card 31

**Q:** Term: Hashing

**A:** Definition: A one-way cryptographic process that creates a unique digital fingerprint (hash) of data.

---

## Card 32

**Q:** Which cryptographic function is recommended for password storage to prevent reverse-engineering?

**A:** bcrypt (or Argon2).

---

## Card 33

**Q:** What is a 'Salt' in the context of password security?

**A:** A random value added to a password before hashing to make identical passwords result in unique hashes.

---

## Card 34

**Q:** The 'hybrid' encryption model used by TLS and HTTPS uses asymmetric encryption for _____.

**A:** Key exchange.

---

## Card 35

**Q:** What is the role of a Certificate Authority (CA) in a PKI system?

**A:** To sign digital certificates and verify that a public key belongs to a specific identity.

---

## Card 36

**Q:** Which version of the TLS protocol is currently the recommended standard with mandatory 'forward secrecy'?

**A:** TLS 1.3.

---

## Card 37

**Q:** Term: Forward Secrecy

**A:** Definition: A security property where compromised long-term keys do not reveal the keys used in past sessions.

---

## Card 38

**Q:** What does E2EE (End-to-End Encryption) guarantee?

**A:** That only the sender and the receiver can read the content, not even the service provider.

---

## Card 39

**Q:** The EU's main data protection regulation, applicable in Norway via the EEA, is known as _____.

**A:** GDPR.

---

## Card 40

**Q:** The principle of collecting only the personal data that is strictly necessary for a specific purpose is called _____.

**A:** Data minimization (dataminimering).

---

## Card 41

**Q:** The principle that data protection must be integrated into systems from the start is called Privacy by _____.

**A:** Design.

---

## Card 42

**Q:** Under GDPR, the entity that decides the 'purpose and means' for processing personal data is the _____.

**A:** Data Controller (behandlingsansvarlig).

---

## Card 43

**Q:** A third party that processes personal data on behalf of a controller is called a _____.

**A:** Data Processor (databehandler).

---

## Card 44

**Q:** What is a 'Data Processor Agreement' (databehandleravtale)?

**A:** A contract regulating how a third party handles personal data on behalf of an organization.

---

## Card 45

**Q:** Within how many hours must a significant personal data breach be reported to the Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet)?

**A:** 72 hours.

---

## Card 46

**Q:** Which role is mandatory for all public bodies and some large private enterprises to ensure GDPR compliance?

**A:** Data Protection Officer (personvernombud).

---

## Card 47

**Q:** Term: Trojan

**A:** Definition: Malware that disguises itself as legitimate software to gain unauthorized access to a system.

---

## Card 48

**Q:** A network of compromised computers controlled by an attacker, often used for DDoS attacks, is a _____.

**A:** Botnet.

---

## Card 49

**Q:** What is a 'Zero-day' vulnerability?

**A:** A software flaw that is unknown to the vendor and has no available patch.

---

## Card 50

**Q:** Term: DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)

**A:** Definition: An attack that floods a service with traffic from multiple sources to make it unavailable.

---

## Card 51

**Q:** What is the difference between phishing and spear phishing?

**A:** Phishing is broad and generic, while spear phishing targets a specific individual or organization.

---

## Card 52

**Q:** Social engineering via SMS is commonly referred to as _____.

**A:** Smishing.

---

## Card 53

**Q:** What is 'Pretexting' in the context of social engineering?

**A:** Creating a fabricated scenario (a pretext) to trick a victim into revealing information.

---

## Card 54

**Q:** The term for high-level, persistent, and often state-sponsored cyber attacks is _____.

**A:** APT (Advanced Persistent Threat).

---

## Card 55

**Q:** What does the 'OWASP Top 10' list represent?

**A:** The ten most critical web application security risks.

---

## Card 56

**Q:** In the NSM risk assessment method, 'secondary values' central to the document are _____.

**A:** ICT systems.

---

## Card 57

**Q:** What is 'Hard drive encryption' intended to protect against?

**A:** Unauthorized access to data if a computer is physically lost or stolen.

---

## Card 58

**Q:** Name one example of a built-in hard drive encryption tool for Windows.

**A:** BitLocker.

---

## Card 59

**Q:** Name one example of a built-in hard drive encryption tool for MacOS.

**A:** FileVault.

---

## Card 60

**Q:** According to the materials, why is encryption critical for GDPR compliance?

**A:** It is considered an appropriate technical measure to protect personal data from unauthorized access.

---

## Card 61

**Q:** What does 'Integrity' mean in the context of cyber security?

**A:** Ensuring that data has not been altered or corrupted by unauthorized parties.

---

## Card 62

**Q:** The process of reducing the risk by implementing security controls is known as risk _____.

**A:** Mitigation (or handling).

---

## Card 63

**Q:** Why is 'logging' important according to NSM's principles?

**A:** It provides a baseline for normal state and allows for the detection of deviations or attacks.

---

## Card 64

**Q:** A false alarm where legitimate traffic is incorrectly flagged as a threat is called a/an _____.

**A:** False positive.

---

## Card 65

**Q:** Which Norwegian authority is responsible for national preventative security and ICT security principles?

**A:** NSM (Nasjonal sikkerhetsmyndighet).

---

## Card 66

**Q:** Which center operated by NSM provides 24/7 incident response and alerts?

**A:** NCSC (Norwegian Cyber Security Centre).

---

## Card 67

**Q:** The right of an individual to have their personal data deleted is known as the 'Right to _____'.

**A:** Be forgotten (or erasure).

---

## Card 68

**Q:** Which attack type involves an attacker sitting between two parties to eavesdrop on or alter communication?

**A:** Man-in-the-Middle (MitM).

---

## Card 69

**Q:** The standard cryptographic hash function currently recommended by NSM to replace MD5 and SHA-1 is _____.

**A:** SHA-256.

---

## Card 70

**Q:** What happens if a firewall is configured with a 'default-allow' policy?

**A:** All traffic is permitted unless it is specifically on a block list.

---

## Card 71

**Q:** In the event of an ICT security breach, which group is responsible for prioritizing and handling the risk reduction strategy?

**A:** Executive management (Ledelsen).

---

## Card 72

**Q:** What is the main goal of Category 4 (Handle and recover) in NSM's principles?

**A:** To limit the impact of an incident and return to normal operations quickly.

---

## Card 73

**Q:** A host-based firewall, such as Windows Defender Firewall, protects _____.

**A:** The individual computer it is running on.

---

## Card 74

**Q:** In cyber security, 'CIA' stands for Confidentiality, Integrity, and _____.

**A:** Availability.

---

## Card 75

**Q:** What is the primary risk of using end-of-life (EOL) software?

**A:** It no longer receives security updates, leaving known vulnerabilities open to exploit.

---

## Card 76

**Q:** A 'Next-Generation Firewall' (NGFW) differs from a traditional firewall by identifying traffic based on _____ rather than just ports.

**A:** Applications and user identity.

---

## Card 77

**Q:** Term: Phishing

**A:** Definition: An attempt to trick users into providing sensitive info by pretending to be a legitimate entity.

---

## Card 78

**Q:** What is the difference between a vulnerability and a threat?

**A:** A vulnerability is a weakness in a system; a threat is an actor or event that can exploit that weakness.

---

## Card 79

**Q:** The 'Supply Chain' attack vector involves gaining access to a target through _____.

**A:** Their third-party vendors or service providers.

---

## Card 80

**Q:** Under GDPR, what does the principle of 'Accuracy' require?

**A:** That personal data must be correct and kept up to date.

---

## Card 81

**Q:** What is the 'Accountability' principle in GDPR?

**A:** The obligation of an organization to demonstrate that they comply with the data protection principles.

---

## Card 82

**Q:** Why is MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication) a critical defense against phishing?

**A:** It requires a second form of verification even if the attacker steals the password.

---

## Card 83

**Q:** Which security measure is described as protecting data when a computer is powered off?

**A:** Hard drive encryption.

---

## Card 84

**Q:** In the NSM principles, 'Mapping users and access needs' is part of which category?

**A:** Category 1: Identify and map.

---

## Card 85

**Q:** What is the purpose of 'Rate limiting' in a WAF?

**A:** To prevent DDoS attacks by limiting the number of requests from a single source.

---

## Card 86

**Q:** The practice of 'Godkjentlisting' (Whitelisting) in NSM's principles refers to _____.

**A:** Allowing only pre-approved software or applications to run on a system.

---
